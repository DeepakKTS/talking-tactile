Why How it works Details Status

Textbook figures a blind student can touch, and that talk back.

A teacher prints a figure as a raised sheet and sets a phone above it. The student explores with one finger. Rest on a part and the phone says what it is. Ask a question out loud and the answer comes from that same figure.

A college science textbook can have a picture on every other page. Very few of those pictures are ever made into something a blind student can touch.

Mostly it comes down to time, because each one is made by hand.

How it works

The teacher prepares a sheet once. After that, the student can come back to it as often as they like.

Making the sheet

What the teacher does

  1. Upload a figureMuse Spark looks at it and lists the parts, a few words for each.
  2. Each part gets an outlineSAM 3.1 traces every part without being trained on that figure first.
  3. Check the resultAnything the models got wrong can be corrected before the figure is published.
  4. Print itThe sheet follows BANA spacing and texture guidelines, and comes with a map of where every part sits.

Reading the sheet

What the student does

  1. Set the sheet under a phoneThe camera watches the hand and works out where the fingertip lands on the page.
  2. Touch a partThe phone says its name.
  3. Ask about itMuse Voice Transcribe listens, using the figure’s own labels as hints for unusual words. Muse Spark answers from this figure only.

The choices behind it

The page stays clean

Earlier talking tactile systems asked for something extra on every sheet: a QR code, color tags, or a tablet underneath. Here the paper carries only the figure.

The teacher has the last word

Models make mistakes with diagrams. Nothing reaches a student until a person has looked at the parts and fixed what needs fixing.

Rules come from the guidelines

I have no background in tactile graphics, so the spacing and texture rules are taken from BANA’s published guidelines, and each sheet gets a report on how well it follows them.

Answers stay on the page

Ask about the left ventricle on a heart diagram and the answer is about that diagram, built from its own parts and labels.

A phone first

An ordinary phone camera is enough today. Later, the same idea should move to the camera in Meta’s glasses.

Where it stands
